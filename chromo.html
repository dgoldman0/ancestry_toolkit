About This Tool

  

    

      

        GEDmatch admixture calculators can report a single overall proportion for each
        population, but chromosome-by-chromosome output exposes how that signal is
        distributed across the genome. This tool reduces those 22 chromosome values
        into a small set of descriptive metrics.
      

      

        The metrics summarize between-chromosome distribution. They do not recover
        tract length, exact admixture dates, or literal ancestry segments. They are
        best read as exploratory signals that can be compared within a run and checked
        against other evidence.
      


      
Parameters

      
    
        
  • Top-n Mean: controls how many of the largest chromosome values contribute to Pulse. n=1 uses the maximum chromosome value.
    • 
        
  • Weighting: when enabled, Mean, CV, and Top-n Mean use SNP counts as chromosome-length proxies. Sparsity remains based on the displayed chromosome percentages.
    • 
      


      
Returned Metrics

      
    
        
  • Mean (%): overall ancestry share across chromosomes, analogous to admixture proportion.
    • 
        
  • Pulse: excess top-chromosome concentration, computed from ((Top-n / Mean) - 1) / log2(100 / Mean). An even row has a natural baseline near zero.
    • 
        
  • Stability: raw chromosome-level smoothness, computed as -log(CV + eps). Higher values mean lower relative variation across chromosomes.
    • 
        
  • Residual Stability: the part of Stability left after fitting Stability against log(Mean + eps). This is a secondary diagnostic for "smoother than expected for its mean."
    • 
        
  • Sparsity: Hoyer sparsity across chromosome percentages. Higher values mean the signal is concentrated on fewer chromosomes.
    • 
        
  • Spike: the raw Top-n / Mean ratio before the excess-Pulse adjustment.
    • 
      


      
Standardized Scores & Chart

      

        Pulse, Stability, Residual Stability, and Sparsity are Yeo-Johnson transformed and
        standardized into z-scores within the current run:
      

      
    
        
  • Pulse Score (z): plotted on the X-axis; higher values mean stronger excess top-heavy concentration.
    • 
        
  • Stability Score (z): plotted on the Y-axis; higher values mean smoother chromosome-level distribution.
    • 
        
  • Residual Stability Score (z): shown in the score table as a mean-adjusted smoothness diagnostic.
    • 
        
  • Sparsity Score (z): encoded in color; blue is less sparse, black is near average, and red is more sparse.
    • 
        
  • Mean (%): sets bubble area; larger bubbles represent greater total ancestry share.
    • 
      

      

        Scores are relative to the populations in the pasted dataset. A point high
        on Stability is not absolutely "old"; it is smoother compared
        with the other components in that run.
      


      
Theoretical Interpretation

      

        Recombination, drift, inheritance randomness, reference-panel overlap, and
        calculator noise can all shape chromosome-level values. The tool therefore
        describes patterns rather than assigning dates:
      

      
    
        
  • High Pulse: one or several chromosomes are elevated relative to the component's own mean.
    • 
        
  • High Stability: the component is more evenly spread across chromosomes.
    • 
        
  • High Residual Stability: the component is more evenly spread than expected for its mean.
    • 
        
  • High Sparsity: ancestry mass is concentrated on fewer chromosomes.
    • 
        
  • Low Mean: interpretations should be cautious, because tiny components are more vulnerable to noise.
    • 
      


      
Limitations

      
    
        
  • Scores are standardized within the dataset, so interpretation is relative, not absolute.
    • 
        
  • Pulse is a between-chromosome concentration statistic, not a date estimate.
    • 
        
  • Weighting by SNP counts approximates chromosome length but does not replace a recombination map.
    • 
        
  • Signals in closely related reference populations can move together; interpretation must consider correlated ancestry panels.
    • 
        
  • Calculator percentages are estimates, not directly observed ancestry tracts.
